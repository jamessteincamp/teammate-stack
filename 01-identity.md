# 01 — Identity

*Who you are, at the level that stays true for months. This is the file the AI reads first, so it should answer "who am I working for?" in a page.*

> **How to fill this in.** Write in plain English, first person. Resist the CV. The useful facts are the ones that change how someone should work with you — what you're actually trying to do, what you're optimizing for, and what you refuse to trade away. If a line wouldn't change the AI's behavior, it probably doesn't belong here.

**Name:** [Your name — or the name you want the AI to use]

**Role, plain English:** [What you do, said the way you'd say it to a friend, not a recruiter. Include how you got here if the path shapes how you think — e.g. "came up through craft, not a management track."]

**The strands of your work, in priority order:**

1. **[The thinker / the researcher / the writer]** — [the core intellectual project, if you have one. Name it plainly.]
2. **[The builder]** — [the systems, tools, or side projects you keep running.]
3. **[The professional]** — [the day job. It's fine for this to be third. If you keep a separate AI setup for work, say so here so the two don't bleed into each other.]

*The ordering matters more than the labels. It tells the AI which thing to protect when they compete for the same hour.*

**Background that shapes the thinking:** [Two or three lines. Education, formative work, the odd detour. Not for credentials — for the AI to understand which analogies will land and which frames you'll reach for.]

**Values:** [A short list, plainly stated. One example from a real stack: "If it's worth doing, it's worth doing right." Include the ones you fail at; the honesty is the point.]

**Great work has two registers:**

- *Private work* answers only to you. [Name the things you do for reflection and learning — the hobby, the collection, the workshop project — that are not for anyone's validation.]
- *Shared work* must be useful to other people. [Say what you enjoy sharing, and what kills the enjoyment. A common answer: sharing dies when it becomes expected, unappreciated, or exploited.]

**Audience:** [Who your thinking is for. "Mostly myself" is a legitimate answer and changes a great deal about how the AI should behave — it removes the pressure to publish, polish, or perform.]

**Working constraint:** [The dominant constraint on your time and attention. Be specific — "about an hour a day of real deep-work time" is more useful than "busy." State the consequence: e.g. "every tool must reduce inboxes, not add them."]

**The AI collaborator I want:** [One paragraph. The relationship, not the feature list. A worked example from a real stack: "A sparring partner, not a validator. Lead with the critique; challenge my reasoning before complimenting it. Don't edit my notes — I rework them myself. Give me material to react to, not finished products."]

## Private facts

> **Optional, and yours to decide.** Some facts about your situation — things you'd tell a close colleague but not a room — materially change how an AI should collaborate with you, but should never appear in anything the AI produces. If you have such facts, this is where they go, with an explicit fence.
>
> The pattern that works: state the fact, then state *what it means for the AI in practice*, then state the fence. For example:
>
> *"[Fact.] What this means for the AI: [the concrete behaviors it should adopt because of it]. This fact never leaves the stack, never appears in an artifact, never becomes framing."*
>
> The behaviors then get their own numbered rules in `05-rules.md`, phrased without the reason. The reason stays here.
