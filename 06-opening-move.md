# 06 — Opening Move

*The default way to start a session. The goal is to trigger the right collaboration pattern, not to write the perfect prompt.*

> **How to fill this in.** Decide what your commonest session actually is, and make that the default opener. Then write one variant for each other kind of session you really have — not the ones you imagine having. Each opener should carry four things: the outcome, the audience, the definition of success, and the collaboration mode. Everything else is already in the stack.

## Default opener — [name your commonest mode]

*A worked example, where the commonest outcome is sharper thinking and the audience is the writer:*

> Spar with me on **[IDEA / QUESTION]**. Success looks like my position stress-tested — weakest premise named, best counterargument on the table — and, if the idea survives, a draft note to my inbox. Read the stack first. Ask only the questions that change the result.

## Variants

**Think-aloud mode** *(for people who compose by talking)*

> I'm going to think out loud about **[X]**. Don't interrupt with answers. Capture, then reflect the structure back to me and name the gaps.

**Retrieval mode** *(reconstructing an event, a source, or a fact trail)*

> Help me reconstruct **[EVENT / TOPIC]**. I'll bring fragments. Give me candidate facts with provenance, and keep confirmed clearly separated from plausible.

**Synthesis mode**

> Sweep **[my inbox / my notes on X]** for surprise edges — connections I haven't made. One good unexpected edge beats three summaries. Don't edit the notes.

**Build mode**

> I want to build **[TOOL / WORKFLOW]**. Success looks like a working first version I can test today — and it must reduce an inbox, not add one.

**Draft mode**

> Draft **[ASSET]** in my voice — 02 and 03 apply hard. Success: it reads well aloud and is ready for me to finesse.

## Why this works

Each opener carries the outcome, the audience, the definition of success, and the collaboration mode. Because the stack holds everything else, the opener can be one sentence and still start the session in the right place.
